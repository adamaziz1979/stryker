1. Company Overview

Stryker was founded in 1941 by orthopaedic surgeon Dr Homer Stryker after he developed medical products to meet unmet patient needs. It is now an S&P 500 medical-technology company listed on the NYSE under SYK. Its core reporting segments are MedSurg & Neurotechnology and Orthopaedics, with businesses spanning instruments, endoscopy, medical equipment, vascular, neuro cranial, knees, hips, trauma and extremities, and enabling technologies.

2. How Stryker Became What It Is Today

Origins → scale
Innovation began with Dr Homer Stryker's inventions for patient care. Over decades the company scaled into a diversified medical-device business.
2013: Robotics
MAKO introduced a robotic-assisted surgical platform into the portfolio. The $1.65bn acquisition helped turn joint replacement into a technology-enabled ecosystem rather than an implant-only proposition.
2020: Extremities
Wright Medical broadened Stryker's trauma and extremities position, including upper and lower extremities and biologics.
2022: Digital workflow
Vocera expanded Stryker into digital clinical communication and care coordination, explicitly supporting Stryker's digital ambitions.
2025: Vascular + focus
Inari Medical established a peripheral vascular platform, while the sale of the U.S. spinal implants business sharpened portfolio focus and retained selected enabling technologies/relationships.
2026: Execution + integration
Stryker entered 2026 with new operating leadership, a reorganised Orthopaedics reporting structure, recovery from a cyber incident, and continuing integration and supply challenges in parts of the portfolio.

Briefing — Stryker's Gallup / Psychometric Assessment

Observed Fact Stryker's current recruitment guidance includes a Gallup interview after the hiring-manager conversation. Stryker describes it as a professional strengths assessment conducted through a web-based platform or phone with a trained analyst, using strengths-themed questions. It can last roughly 30 minutes to 1.5 hours, after which feedback is shared with the recruiter and hiring manager.

What it is — and what it is not

Supported by evidenceDo not assume
It is structured around recurring talents/strengths and natural patterns of thought, feeling and behaviour.That it is a numerical reasoning or IQ test.
Stryker and Gallup have a decades-long strengths and talent-selection relationship.That the hiring assessment is simply the standard CliftonStrengths questionnaire.
Historical Gallup material describes Stryker role-specific, validated talent profiles and standardised questioning.That today's scoring, thresholds or exact role profile are identical to the 2005 methodology.
Stryker's advice is to be yourself rather than manufacture an ideal persona.That there is a public list of “correct” answers for this Senior Lead role.

Likely experience

The interview may feel more structured and less conversational than a normal competency interview. Candidate accounts often describe relatively fast questioning and limited feedback. The assessment is designed to identify consistent behavioural patterns rather than reward polished long-form answers.

Preparation for this Senior Lead role

Working Hypothesis Based on the job specification — not on a disclosed Stryker scoring profile — likely relevant themes include initiative, ownership, achievement orientation, influence, relationship building, resilience, curiosity/learning, problem solving and comfort operating in ambiguity.

Prepare evidence, not scripts Have 8–10 real examples mentally ready: a result you personally drove; challenging a prevailing view; senior-stakeholder influence; resistance; ownership outside remit; setback/recovery; mentoring; an opportunity you identified before being asked; a difficult decision with incomplete information; and measurable business value.
Answer naturally and consistently Keep answers direct. Do not try to reverse-engineer a “perfect Stryker personality”. A structured assessment can expose inconsistent impression-management across repeated themes.

Recruitment sequence

Stryker's published process is broadly: Recruiter → Hiring Manager → Gallup assessment → Final-stage interviews → Selection/offer. Stryker states that after the Gallup stage, the recruiter and hiring manager decide on next steps.
